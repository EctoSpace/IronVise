EU AI Act Article 12 - record-keeping

How the IronVise event schema maps to the logging requirement. Technical mapping, not legal advice.

Applies from 2 December 2027 (Annex III stand-alone high-risk systems), not 2 August 2026. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 - the Digital Omnibus on AI - entered into force 27 July 2026 and deferred the Chapter III Section 2 obligations. Annex I embedded systems: 2 August 2028.

The part the Act does not specify

Article 12 requires logs be recorded; Article 19 requires they be kept. Neither requires they be tamper-evident. A plain application log satisfies a literal reading while remaining editable by the provider who is the subject of the audit.
  • Hash chaining - canonical v2 input binds session_id and created_at into the digest; editing any event breaks every later link.
  • Ed25519 signatures - per-session key pinned in session_public_key, optionally a did:key:.
  • External anchoring - OpenTimestamps bounds when the log existed, outside the provider's control.
  • Offline verification - verify-cert checks an exported .elc with no database and no network.

Limits - read before citing

  • Proves: the log has not been altered since it was written, and existed no later than the anchor.
  • Does not prove: that the log is complete, or that what was written was true. A hash chain constrains what happens after an event is appended - not an event never appended.
  • IronVise only logs what runs through IronVise.
  • A certificate is supporting evidence, not compliance. Article 12 is one requirement among many in Chapter III.
  • Organisations get certified; software does not.
  • Not legal advice, and not reviewed by counsel.
