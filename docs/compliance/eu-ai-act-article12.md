# EU AI Act Article 12 - record-keeping, and what IronVise maps to it

**Status of this document:** a technical mapping, written by the IronVise
maintainers, of Article 12 and Article 19 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 onto a
concrete ledger schema. It is **not legal advice** and it is **not a conformity
assessment**. See [Limits](#7-limits) - please read that section before citing
anything here.

Last verified against primary sources: **2026-08-10**.

---

## 1. When this applies

Article 12 sits in Chapter III Section 2 (requirements for high-risk AI
systems). Those obligations were originally to apply from 2 August 2026. They
no longer do.

**Regulation (EU) 2026/1744** - the "Digital Omnibus on AI" - was published in
the Official Journal on 24 July 2026 and entered into force on 27 July 2026,
six days before the deadline it postponed:

| Scope | Originally | Now applies from |
|---|---|---|
| Annex III stand-alone high-risk systems | 2 Aug 2026 | **2 December 2027** |
| Annex I high-risk embedded in regulated products | 2 Aug 2027 | **2 August 2028** |

What *did* take effect on 2 August 2026 is Article 50 (transparency:
AI-interaction disclosure, synthetic-content marking, deepfake notices).
Article 50 concerns disclosure and watermarking, **not** logging, and nothing in
this document addresses it.

So: Article 12 is not yet enforceable. The engineering problem it describes is
nonetheless real today, and the standards that will define *how* to satisfy it
are being written now (§6).

## 2. What Article 12 requires

> **Article 12(1).** High-risk AI systems shall technically allow for the
> automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system.

> **Article 12(2).** In order to ensure a level of traceability of the
> functioning of a high-risk AI system that is appropriate to the intended
> purpose of the system, logging capabilities shall enable the recording of
> events relevant for:
>
> (a) identifying situations that may result in the high-risk AI system
> presenting a risk within the meaning of Article 79(1) or in a substantial
> modification;
>
> (b) facilitating the post-market monitoring referred to in Article 72; and
>
> (c) monitoring the operation of high-risk AI systems referred to in
> Article 26(5).

> **Article 12(3).** For high-risk AI systems referred to in point 1 (a), of
> Annex III, the logging capabilities shall provide, at a minimum:
>
> (a) recording of the period of each use of the system (start date and time and
> end date and time of each use);
> (b) the reference database against which input data has been checked by the
> system;
> (c) the input data for which the search has led to a match;
> (d) the identification of the natural persons involved in the verification of
> the results, as referred to in Article 14(5).

## 3. What Article 19 requires

> **Article 19(1).** Providers of high-risk AI systems shall keep the logs
> referred to in Article 12(1), automatically generated by their high-risk AI
> systems, to the extent such logs are under their control. Without prejudice to
> applicable Union or national law, the logs shall be kept for a period
> appropriate to the intended purpose of the high-risk AI system, of at least six
> months, unless provided otherwise in the applicable Union or national law, in
> particular in Union law on the protection of personal data.

> **Article 19(2).** Providers that are financial institutions subject to
> requirements regarding their internal governance, arrangements or processes
> under Union financial services law shall maintain the logs automatically
> generated by their high-risk AI systems as part of the documentation kept
> under the relevant financial services law.

## 4. Mapping to the IronVise schema

Field names below are exact and refer to [`docs/reference/schema.md`](../reference/schema.md).
Event-kind names are the serde `snake_case` renderings of the `EventPayload`
enum in `crates/host/src/schema.rs`.

| Requirement | IronVise mechanism | Exact fields | Coverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **12(1)** automatic recording of events over the lifetime | Append-only `agent_events`; one row per agent step, written by the loop, not by the model | `sequence` (strict order, unique), `created_at` (µs precision), `payload` (JSONB), `session_id` | **Partial** - see 12(1) note below |
| **12(1)** logs cannot be quietly rewritten | DB-level immutability: no `UPDATE`/`DELETE` in application code, enforced by a `prevent_updates()` trigger | - | Full, within the database |
| **12(2)(a)** situations presenting a risk | Guard denials, tripwire rejections and policy refusals are appended to the chain before the action is executed | `guard_denial` (`action`, `reason`, `enforced`); `tripwire_rejection` (`action`, `reason`, `stage`, `enforced`) | **Full** for runs that pass through IronVise |
| **12(2)(a)** substantial modification | The governing policy is hashed and pinned per session; a changed policy yields a different hash | `agent_sessions.policy_hash` | Full for policy changes |
| **12(2)(a)** signing-key changes | Dedicated event kinds | `key_rotation`, `key_revocation` | Full |
| **12(2)(b)** post-market monitoring (Art. 72) | Per-run session records plus execution outcomes; optional SIEM egress in JSON/CEF/LEEF | `agent_sessions.goal`, `.goal_hash`, `.status`, `.llm_backend`, `.llm_model`, `.created_at`, `.finished_at`; `agent_action_log.status`, `.started_at`, `.finished_at`, `.error_msg`; `webhooks.siem_format` | Full for runs that pass through IronVise |
| **12(2)(c)** monitoring operation (Art. 26(5)) | Action lifecycle and human-in-the-loop decisions | `agent_action_log.*`; `pending_approvals.gate_id`, `.action_name`, `.approved`, `.reason`, `.decided_at`; event kinds `approval_required`, `approval_decision` | Full for runs that pass through IronVise |
| **12(2)** traceability of what a result was based on | Data receipts: after each tool call, SHA-256 of what it returned, per text, never the data | `data_receipt` (`tool`, `source`, `call_id`, `response_sha256`, `text_sha256`, `text_bytes`) | **Supporting** for runs through the Claude Code hook or the MCP proxy: identifies the data without keeping it; whoever holds the data checks it with `ironvise data-receipts --file` |
| **12(3)(a)** period of each use | Session start and end timestamps | `agent_sessions.created_at`, `.finished_at` | Full |
| **12(3)(b)** reference database checked | - | - | **None** - not a biometric system |
| **12(3)(c)** input data producing a match | - | - | **None** - not a biometric system |
| **12(3)(d)** natural persons verifying results | Human approval decisions are recorded, but identity is by gate and decision, not by verified natural-person identity | `pending_approvals.gate_id`, `.approved`, `.reason`; `approval_decision` | **Partial** |
| **19(1)** retention ≥ 6 months | Events persist in PostgreSQL or SQLite; nothing is auto-deleted | `agent_events.created_at` | **Deployment responsibility** - not enforced by the software |
| **19(2)** financial-services documentation | Export as `.elc` certificate or W3C Verifiable Credential for filing alongside other records | - | Supporting evidence only |

### Notes on the partials - read these, not just the table

**12(1) "over the lifetime of the system".** IronVise records the lifetime of
*sessions that run through it*. It is not a system-wide recorder. If a
high-risk AI system has code paths that do not go through IronVise, Article
12(1) is not satisfied for those paths by anything here.

**12(2)(a) is structured.** When the Guard denies an intent, the loop appends a
`guard_denial` event (`EventPayload::GuardDenial`) with `action`, `reason`, and
`enforced`. Tripwire and policy refusals append `tripwire_rejection` with a
`stage` of `prompt_scan`, `validation`, or `policy`. Both are written before
execution and are enumerable without grepping free text.

`guard_denial` and `tripwire_rejection` are also **webhook egress labels**
(`EgressKind` in `crates/host/src/webhook.rs`), matching the ledger event kinds
so SIEM filters and ledger queries use the same vocabulary.

An auditor can therefore enumerate risk events programmatically from the ledger
today. Older sessions written before this typed shape may still contain
`Thought { content: "Guard denied: …" }` prose; new sessions do not.

**12(3) is largely out of scope.** Annex III point 1(a) is remote biometric
identification. IronVise performs no biometric matching, so (b) and (c) have
no analogue. Do not present this mapping as covering a biometric deployment.

## 5. The part the Act does not specify: integrity

Article 12 requires that logs be *recorded*. Article 19 requires that they be
*kept*. **Neither requires that they be tamper-evident**, and neither specifies
a format, a signing scheme, or how a third party could detect after-the-fact
editing.

That is a real gap, not a rhetorical one. A plain application log satisfies a
literal reading of Articles 12 and 19 while remaining trivially editable by
anyone with database access - including the provider who is the subject of the
audit.

What IronVise adds on top of the requirement:

- **Hash chaining.** Each row's `previous_hash` must equal the previous row's
  `content_hash`. The canonical v2 input is
  `"ELv2\0" previous_hash \0 sequence \0 payload_json \0 session_id \0 created_at`,
  so the timestamp and the owning session are bound into the digest and cannot
  be altered independently of it. Editing or reordering any event breaks every
  link after it.
- **Per-session Ed25519 signatures.** `agent_event_signatures` stores
  `signature` and `public_key` against the `content_hash`; the key is pinned in
  `agent_sessions.session_public_key` and optionally expressed as a `did:key:`
  in `session_did`. A rewritten event would need a valid signature under the
  session key.
- **External timestamping.** The chain tip can be timestamped via
  OpenTimestamps, which bounds *when* the log existed by evidence outside the
  provider's control.
- **Offline verification.** `verify-certificate` and the standalone
  `verify-cert` binary check an exported `.elc` without a database and without
  network access, so an auditor is not asked to trust the producer's server.

### What that does and does not prove

It proves: **this log has not been altered since it was written**, and it
existed no later than the anchor.

It does not prove: that the log is **complete**, or that what was written was
**true**. A hash chain constrains what can happen *after* an event is appended.
It says nothing about an event that was never appended, or about a component
that lied to the recorder before the record was made. Anyone claiming otherwise
about any append-only ledger - this one included - is overselling.

## 6. Standards in flight

Neither of the two standards that will govern this area is final. Both are worth
tracking; neither should be cited as settled.

| Standard | Body | Status (2026-08-10) |
|---|---|---|
| **prEN 18229-1** - *AI trustworthiness framework, Part 1: Logging, transparency and human oversight* | CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 WG4 | **Public enquiry open until 20 August 2026.** Supports Articles 12, 13 and 14. |
| **ISO/IEC 24970** - *Artificial intelligence - AI system logging* | ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 | **FDIS** (final-draft ballot); advanced past the DIS comment stage. |

prEN 18229-1 Part 1 is the European standard directly covering Article 12
logging. Once harmonised standards are cited in the Official Journal, conformity
with them confers a presumption of conformity with the corresponding
requirements. Until then, any mapping - including this one - is an engineering
argument, not a compliance guarantee.

## 7. Limits

Stated bluntly, because the value of this document depends on it being accurate
rather than flattering.

1. **IronVise only logs what runs through IronVise.** It is not an
   organisation-wide logging solution and cannot attest to systems it does not
   mediate.
2. **This mapping is not legal advice.** It has not been reviewed by counsel. It
   is a technical reading of the text by the people who wrote the ledger.
3. **A certificate is supporting evidence, not compliance.** Article 12 is one
   requirement among many in Chapter III. Verifying an `.elc` file proves the
   integrity of a log; it says nothing about risk management (Art. 9), data
   governance (Art. 10), technical documentation (Art. 11), transparency
   (Art. 13), or human oversight (Art. 14).
4. **Only organisations get certified, not software.** Consistent with
   [`docs/compliance/iso42001.md`](iso42001.md): no tool can make a provider compliant, and
   no vendor can sell a conformity assessment.
5. **Article 12(2)(a) coverage is structured** for new sessions (§4); older
   prose denials may still appear in pre-typed ledgers.
6. **Retention is not enforced** by the software; Article 19's six-month floor
   is a deployment decision.
7. **The delayed date is a fact, not a reprieve.** 2 December 2027 is the
   application date for Annex III systems; conformity work and standards
   adoption run well ahead of it.
